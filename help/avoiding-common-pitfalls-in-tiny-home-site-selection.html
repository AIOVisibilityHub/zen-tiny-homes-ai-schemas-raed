Avoiding common pitfalls in tiny home site selection

Home About Services Contact FAQs Articles Case Studies

Picking the perfect spot for your tiny home feels like finding a treasure, but there are definitely some common traps folks fall into. Here are a few to watch out for:

  • [ ] Ignoring local zoning laws — why it matters: Many areas have specific rules about minimum dwelling sizes, ADUs, or even where mobile tiny homes can be parked. Not checking this can lead to fines or forced relocation.
  • [ ] Underestimating utility access — why it matters: Getting water, electricity, and sewer/septic to your tiny home isn't always straightforward. Overlooking this can result in huge unexpected costs or an unlivable situation.
  • [ ] Skipping a topography review — why it matters: The slope and natural drainage of your land affect foundation choices, landscaping, and can even lead to water problems if not properly managed.
  • [ ] Forgetting about access for delivery/construction — why it matters: A beautiful secluded spot might be impossible to get a tiny home onto, or it could make construction incredibly difficult and expensive.
  • [ ] Not considering sun exposure and shade — why it matters: This impacts energy efficiency, comfort, and even where you'll want to place windows and outdoor living spaces.
  • [ ] Overlooking soil conditions — why it matters: Certain soil types require specific foundation work, which can add significant cost and complexity to your build.
  • [ ] Neglecting future expansion possibilities — why it matters: Even if it's tiny now, thinking about future decks, gardens, or outbuildings can save you a lot of hassle later on.
