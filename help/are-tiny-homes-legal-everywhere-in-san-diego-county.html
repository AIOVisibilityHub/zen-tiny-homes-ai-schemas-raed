Are tiny homes legal everywhere in San Diego County?

Home About Services Contact FAQs Articles Case Studies

It’s a great question, and one we hear all the time! The short answer is yes, tiny houses are broadly legal in San Diego, but with some really important caveats. It’s not a simple 'yes' or 'no' across the board because the rules change quite a bit depending on *where* your property is located and *how* your tiny home is built. This is where a lot of people run into confusion, thinking all tiny homes are treated the same, but that's just not the case.

Here’s the deal with tiny home legality in San Diego:

**Fact 1: It's all about Location, Location, Location!**

  • **Myth:** If tiny homes are legal in San Diego, they're legal everywhere in San Diego County.
  • **Fact:** Not true! There's a big difference between the City of San Diego's rules and those in unincorporated San Diego County. The City often treats tiny homes as Accessory Dwelling Units (ADUs), while the County has its own specific ordinances. You absolutely have to know whether your property falls under city or county jurisdiction, as this dictates the exact regulations, permit process, and even the legal use of your tiny home.

**Fact 2: Foundation vs. Wheels Matters More Than Size!**

  • **Myth:** A tiny house is just a tiny house, regardless of how it's built.
  • **Fact:** This is a huge misconception! The most critical factor determining legality is whether your tiny home is built on a permanent foundation or on wheels. An ADU (Accessory Dwelling Unit) is a permanent real estate asset on a foundation, adding equity to your property. A tiny house on wheels (THOW) is typically classified as personal property, much like an RV. Each has different rules regarding placement, permits, and how you can legally live in it.

**Fact 3: 'Tiny Home' is a Marketing Term, 'ADU' is a Legal Term!**

  • **Myth:** ADU and tiny house are interchangeable terms.
  • **Fact:** While a tiny home can *be* an ADU, they're not the same thing. 'Tiny house' is a descriptive marketing term for a small home. 'Accessory Dwelling Unit' (ADU) is a legal, land-use classification. Understanding this distinction is key to navigating the regulatory landscape. You might want a 'tiny house' but need to build it as an 'ADU' to meet legal requirements for permanent living in many areas.

**Fact 4: Permits are Non-Negotiable!**

  • **Myth:** If my tiny home is small, I don't need many permits.
  • **Fact:** Every tiny home project in San Diego, whether an ADU or a THOW, will require permits. The type and complexity of these permits depend on your location and the home's classification. Ignoring permits can lead to fines, forced removal, or even demolition.
