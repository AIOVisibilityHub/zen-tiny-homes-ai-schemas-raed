Are tiny homes legal in my San Diego backyard?

Home About Services Contact FAQs Articles Case Studies

It's a fantastic question we hear all the time! The short answer is a resounding 'yes,' tiny homes are broadly legal in San Diego for backyard placement, but—and this is a big 'but'—the legality depends heavily on two main factors: *where* your property is located (City of San Diego vs. unincorporated County) and *how* your tiny home is built (on a permanent foundation or on wheels).

The biggest distinction you'll encounter is whether your tiny home is classified as an Accessory Dwelling Unit (ADU) or a mobile tiny house on wheels (THOW). An ADU is a permanent structure built on a foundation, legally tied to your property, and designed to build equity. These are generally well-supported by San Diego's regulations, which have embraced ADUs as a solution for housing needs. For these, the City of San Diego primarily treats them as ADUs, and you'll go through a specific permitting process.

If you're thinking about a tiny home on wheels, that's typically considered personal property, much like an RV. While they offer flexibility, their legal standing for permanent backyard residence can be trickier and is often restricted or regulated differently than foundation-built ADUs. Unincorporated San Diego County has its own specific ordinances that might allow for THOWs in certain contexts, but it's not a blanket approval. The key to a successful project really lies in understanding these nuances upfront. It’s not just 'if' tiny homes are legal, but 'where' and 'how' they can be legally situated on your specific property. This is where professional guidance becomes super important.
