Worried About Where to Put Your Tiny Home Legally?

Home About Services Contact FAQs Articles Case Studies

It's totally understandable to feel a bit worried about the legality of placing a tiny home. The rules aren't always crystal clear, and they can vary a lot even within San Diego County! Many people wonder if tiny homes are even allowed, and the good news is, yes, they generally are.

But here's the catch: the 'where' and 'how' are super important. It all hinges on whether your home is built on a permanent foundation or on wheels, and whether you're in the City of San Diego or an unincorporated county area. Getting this right upfront is key to avoiding frustration and ensuring a successful project.

We've spent years helping folks navigate these exact questions.

First off, understand the distinction: a tiny home on a permanent foundation is often treated as an Accessory Dwelling Unit (ADU) in the City of San Diego. These are considered real estate assets, and there are specific zoning definitions for them. If you're looking to build equity and have a legally recognized secondary unit on your property, this is likely your path. The City has worked to make ADUs more accessible, so there's usually a clear process to follow, though it does involve permits and inspections, just like any other construction.

If your tiny home is on wheels, it's generally classified as personal property, much like an RV. In unincorporated San Diego County, there are specific ordinances that might allow for their placement, often with limitations on how long they can stay or how they can be used (e.g., as a guest house vs. primary residence). It's crucial to check with the specific county or city planning department where you intend to place it. These rules can change, and what's permissible in Fallbrook might be different from La Jolla.

Don't try to guess or assume! The most critical step is to really dig into the local regulations for your specific property.
