Myth 1: All tiny homes are treated the same by zoning laws.

Fact: Absolutely not! The biggest misconception we see is people thinking a tiny house on wheels (THOW) has the same legal standing as an Accessory Dwelling Unit (ADU) built on a foundation. An ADU is a permanent real estate asset tied to your property, designed to build equity, with a legal land-use classification. A THOW, however, is often considered personal property, like an RV, and typically offers a flexible lifestyle but might depreciate. Zoning laws treat these very differently, impacting where and how you can place them.

Myth 2: If a property has space, I can put a tiny home there.

Fact: While space is important, it's only one piece of the puzzle. Zoning laws dictate much more than just physical space. They determine allowed uses (residential, commercial, agricultural), density, setbacks from property lines, maximum height, and even how many structures can be on one parcel. Your property's specific zoning designation will be the primary factor in determining if a tiny home is permissible, and under what conditions.

Myth 3: San Diego has banned tiny homes.

Fact: This is a common worry, but it's not true! Tiny homes are broadly legal in San Diego. The catch is that their legality is "highly dependent on where your property is located and how the home is built." The City of San Diego often treats them as ADUs, while unincorporated San Diego County has its own ordinances. Our expertise lies in understanding these nuances and guiding you through the specific requirements for your location.

Myth 4: I can just park a tiny home on wheels anywhere on my land.

Fact: Not quite. While THOWs offer flexibility, even they are subject to regulations. In many areas, they might be considered recreational vehicles and have limitations on long-term occupancy or where they can be parked. If you're looking for a permanent living solution, a foundation-built tiny home, often classified as an ADU, is usually the more straightforward and legal-minded path, offering greater permanence and value.

Myth 5: Getting permits for a tiny home is impossible.

Fact: It can certainly feel daunting, but it's far from impossible! With the right guidance and understanding of the process, permits are completely achievable. This is a question we hear constantly, and it’s totally understandable why there’s confusion! The short answer is a resounding 'yes,' tiny homes are broadly legal in San Diego. But here's the kicker: how they're legal really depends on where your property is located and exactly how the home is built.

It’s not a one-size-fits-all situation. The rules can be quite different between the City of San Diego and the unincorporated areas of San Diego County. The most critical factor is whether your tiny home is built on a permanent foundation or if it’s on wheels.

A tiny home on a permanent foundation is often treated as an Accessory Dwelling Unit (ADU), which has specific zoning and permitting processes. These are legally tied to your property and build equity. If it's on wheels, like a Tiny House on Wheels (THOW), it's typically considered personal property, much like an RV, and has different regulations regarding where it can be parked and for how long.
