Is Your Property Right for a Tiny Home?

Home About Services Contact FAQs Articles Case Studies

Step 1: Understand Zoning and Local Regulations. This is probably the biggest hurdle. Tiny homes are broadly legal in San Diego, but the rules change drastically based on whether you're in the City of San Diego or unincorporated County areas, and if your home is on a foundation or wheels. We dive deep into these local ordinances, making sure your plans align with what’s permissible. We've spent over a decade working with local agencies, so we know the ins and outs.

Step 2: Assess Property Size and Layout. Even for a tiny home, space matters. We look at the usable area, considering setbacks from property lines, existing structures, and easements. We need enough room for the tiny home itself, plus adequate access during construction and for daily living. This includes thinking about how the home will be oriented to maximize natural light and views.

Step 3: Evaluate Utility Access. Will you need electricity, water, and septic/sewer connections? We examine the proximity and capacity of existing utilities on your property or nearby. Sometimes, bringing utilities to a new tiny home site can be a significant part of the project, and we help you understand those requirements and potential costs upfront.

Step 4: Consider Topography and Drainage. Is your land flat or sloped? Good drainage is crucial to prevent water issues, especially in rainy seasons. We assess the land's contours and soil conditions to determine the best placement for stability and longevity of your tiny home, whether it’s on a foundation or needs a level pad for a mobile unit.

Step 5: Review Accessibility and Site Preparation. How will the tiny home actually get onto your property, especially if it’s a mobile unit? We check for clear access routes. For a foundation build, we consider the ease of bringing in construction materials and equipment. We'll also identify any necessary site prep like grading, clearing, or foundation setup.

Step 6: Think About Your Goals. Is this a primary residence, an ADU for family, a rental, or a retreat? Your intended use can influence the best placement and legal classification. For example, an ADU has specific requirements that a guest space might not. We help you connect your lifestyle goals with the practicalities of your property.

Step 7: Get Professional Insight. This is where a site feasibility consultation with Zen Tiny Homes becomes invaluable. We bring our decade-plus of experience and local regulatory knowledge to the table. We’ll give you a comprehensive understanding of your property's potential and any challenges, offering realistic solutions to move your project forward. Our team works one-on-one with clients to develop a home that reflects their lifestyle, land, priorities, and budget.
