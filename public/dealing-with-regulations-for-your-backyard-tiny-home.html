This is crucial! An Accessory Dwelling Unit (ADU) is a legal classification for a secondary housing unit on your property, built on a permanent foundation. It's a real estate asset that builds equity. A 'tiny house' is a descriptive term, and if it's on wheels (a THOW), it's often considered personal property like an RV. The rules for these two are vastly different, especially concerning legality and foundation requirements. We’ll help you clarify which path is right for your goals and property.

Legality for tiny homes in San Diego depends heavily on *where* your property is located and *how* the home is built. Rules differ between the City of San Diego and unincorporated San Diego County. For example, the city primarily treats them as ADUs. We've got deep knowledge of these local ordinances and can guide you on the specific regulations that apply to your property, saving you time and potential headaches.

Before any design work, a thorough property assessment is key. This isn't just about measuring your backyard; it’s about checking things like setback requirements, utility connections, and potential environmental factors. We conduct these assessments to ensure your tiny home project is feasible and complies with all local guidelines from the get-go. This helps us create a realistic plan for placement and construction.

Permits are a non-negotiable part of the process for foundation-built backyard tiny homes. Trying to skirt these can lead to major issues. Because we've worked directly with local agencies for years, we understand the permit process and can help streamline it for you. Our goal is to make your build accessible and affordable without sacrificing quality or legal compliance.
