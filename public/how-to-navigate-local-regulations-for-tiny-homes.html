How to navigate local regulations for tiny homes?

Home About Services Contact FAQs Articles Case Studies

Step 1: Understand Your Tiny Home Type — The first thing to clarify is whether you're planning for an Accessory Dwelling Unit (ADU), which is usually a permanent structure on a foundation, or a mobile tiny house (often called a THOW). The rules are quite different! ADUs are real estate investments, while mobile tiny homes are typically considered personal property. Knowing this distinction from the start is key to understanding which regulations apply to you.

Step 2: Research Local Zoning Codes — Every city and county has its own set of zoning ordinances. These codes dictate where you can place a tiny home, how big it can be, setbacks from property lines, and even aesthetic requirements. You'll need to check if tiny homes are explicitly allowed in your specific zone. Our Site Plan Program helps with this, covering land use planning and zoning details.

Step 3: Consult with Local Planning Departments — It’s always a good idea to speak directly with your local planning or building department. They can provide specific guidelines, necessary permits, and clarify any ambiguities in the zoning codes. Don't be afraid to ask lots of questions – they're there to help! Zen Tiny Homes has a unique perspective from actively working with these departments to expand accessibility for tiny home living.

Step 4: Assess Utility Hookups and Infrastructure — Regulations also cover how your tiny home connects to essential services like water, sewer/septic, and electricity. You’ll need to ensure your property can accommodate these connections, or plan for any necessary upgrades. Our site feasibility consultations include assessing your power and water supply, drainage, and septic needs.

Step 5: Work with an Experienced Builder — This is where Zen Tiny Homes really shines. Our experience at the intersection of design, construction, and tiny-home feasibility means we can guide you through every regulatory hurdle. We help with code planning, blueprints, and ensuring your custom design meets all requirements. We make sure your home is not just beautifully designed but also fully compliant, moving you from dream to a realistic, legal plan.
